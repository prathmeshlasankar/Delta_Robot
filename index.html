How it works

A delta robot is a parallel kinematic mechanism: several arms share the work of positioning one platform.

1 · Three arms, one platform

Three identical arms hang from a fixed triangular frame. Each arm connects to the same moving end-effector.

2 · Parallelogram linkages

Each lower arm is a pair of parallel rods. This keeps the end-effector facing the same way while it moves.

3 · Coordinated motion

An actuator at the top of each arm sets its angle. The three angles together fix the position of the end-effector in space.

Main components

Base / frame

The fixed triangular upper frame that carries the actuators.

Three actuators

One motor per arm, mounted on the frame, sets the arm angle.

Upper arms

Short driven arms that swing from the actuators.

Parallelogram linkages

Pairs of parallel rods joining each upper arm to the end-effector.

End-effector

The moving platform that carries a gripper or tool.

Controller

Electronics and software that command the three actuators together.

Working principle

The conceptual sequence behind every move.

  1. Target positionA point in space the end-effector should reach.
  2. Inverse kinematicsMaths that turns the target point into one angle per arm.
  3. Coordinated arm movementAll three actuators move together to those angles.
  4. Target reachedThe end-effector arrives at the requested point.

The 3D demo above does use a standard delta-robot inverse-kinematics formula, with placeholder arm lengths chosen only for display. Our own kinematic model for the real robot is still future work.
